THE ISSUE

What happens when waste is not managed properly? Improper waste management can affect people, communities, animals, and the environment.

The Problem at Hand

Improper waste management is a serious environmental problem in the Philippines. Poor waste segregation, littering, and improper disposal cause garbage to accumulate in streets, communities, rivers, and other public areas. These practices contribute to air, land, and water pollution. They also clog drainage systems, which increases the risk of flooding during heavy rain. Improperly managed waste threatens wildlife and affects the health, safety, and quality of life of Filipino communities.

The Scale of the Problem

The scale of the waste problem shows why proper segregation, recycling, reuse, collection, and disposal matter.

60.76k tons of waste generated in the Philippines each day
2.7M tons of plastic waste produced each year
20% of plastic waste estimated to enter the ocean

The Causes and Effects of Improper Waste Management

Improper waste management is more than just a cleanliness issue. When waste is not properly reduced, segregated, collected, recycled, or disposed of, it can pollute the environment, affect human health, damage ecosystems, and create problems within communities.

Why Does This Matter?

Every day, homes, schools, businesses, and communities produce waste. In the Philippines, approximately 61,000 metric tons of solid waste are generated every day.

Proper waste management is not only about throwing garbage away. It also includes reducing waste, separating materials correctly, reusing items, recycling, composting, and proper disposal.

Understanding the causes and effects of improper waste management can help people make better decisions about how they handle waste.

What's the Impact?

Cause 1: Improper Waste Segregation


One major cause of poor waste management is not separating waste properly. Biodegradable waste, recyclable materials, residual waste, and special waste should not all be mixed together.


Republic Act No. 9003 requires waste segregation at the source. Proper segregation makes recycling, composting, reuse, and disposal easier.

Effect 1: Pollution of Land, Water, and Air


Improperly discarded waste can pollute the environment. Waste dumped on land can affect soil and water, while burning garbage can release harmful substances into the air.


Plastic waste can also travel through drainage systems and rivers and eventually reach the ocean.

Cause 2: Lack of Awareness


Waste management also depends on people's daily habits. Littering, mixing different types of waste, and not recycling can increase the amount of garbage in the community.


Environmental education can help people understand how to reduce, reuse, recycle, and properly dispose of waste.

Effect 2: Flooding and Community Problems


Garbage that collects in streets, canals, and drainage systems can block the flow of water.


Blocked drains can contribute to flooding and standing water, especially during heavy rain.

Cause 3: Single-Use Plastics


Plastic bags, sachets, wrappers, bottles, and other disposable products are often used only once before they become waste.


The World Bank reports that the Philippines generates around 2.7 million tons of plastic waste each year.

Effect 3: Risks to Human Health


Uncollected garbage can attract insects and rodents. Standing water can also become a place where mosquitoes reproduce.


Unsafe waste handling and burning may also affect the health of workers and nearby communities.

Cause 4: Improper Collection and Disposal


Waste problems can become worse when garbage is not collected regularly or when people dump or burn garbage in unsafe places.


Proper waste management needs both responsible people and a good system for collecting and disposing of waste.

Effect 4: Harm to Wildlife and Ecosystems


Waste that enters rivers, beaches, and oceans can harm animals and damage their habitats.


Plastic pollution can also affect marine ecosystems, fisheries, tourism, and communities that depend on healthy oceans.

From Street to Sea

A plastic wrapper thrown on the street may seem small, but during heavy rain, it can be carried into a drainage canal. When garbage collects in drainage systems, it causes blockages. Waste also travels through rivers and eventually reaches the ocean—proving how a small act of improper disposal creates a much larger environmental problem.
